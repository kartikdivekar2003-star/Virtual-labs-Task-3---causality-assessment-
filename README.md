Objective
To evaluate the relationship between a suspected drug and an adverse drug reaction using standard causality assessment principles and classify the reaction as Certain, Probable, or Possible.

Case Study
Patient Information:
Patient Initials:S.M.
Age:42 years
Gender:Female
Weight:65 kg
Medical History:Hypertension
Known Drug Allergies:No known drug allergies

Medication History
The patient was diagnosed with a urinary tract infection and was prescribed:

Drug:Ciprofloxacin
Dose:500 mg
Route:Oral
Frequency:Twice daily
Indication:Urinary tract infection
Start Date:10/09/2026

concomitant medication 
The patient was also taking amlodipine 5 mg once daily for hypertension, which she had been taking regularly for more than one year without any previous adverse reactions.

Adverse Reaction

On 12/09/2026, approximately 48 hours after starting ciprofloxacin, the patient developed:
Severe nausea
Repeated vomiting
Abdominal discomfort
Loss of appetite
The symptoms became significant enough to interfere with normal food and fluid intake.

No new medication, food, supplement, or cosmetic product had been introduced during this period.

Timeline
10/09/2026 Ciprofloxacin started
11/09/2026 No significant symptoms reported
12/09/2026 Nausea, vomiting and abdominal discomfort developed.
13/09/2026 Ciprofloxacin discontinued after medical evaluation.
14/09/2026 Symptoms substantially improved 
15/09/2026 Patient recovered 

Dechallenge
Ciprofloxacin was discontinued on 13/09/2026.
Following withdrawal of ciprofloxacin, the patient's nausea, vomiting and abdominal discomfort substantially improved within approximately 24 hours.
Dechallenge: Positive
Rechallenge: The patient was not re-exposed to ciprofloxacin after recovery.
Rechallenge: Not performed

Alternative Causes
No new medication, food, supplement, or other relevant exposure was identified around the time of the reaction.

The patient had no reported history of similar gastrointestinal symptoms immediately before starting ciprofloxacin.
Therefore, no strong alternative cause was identified from the available information.

Causality Assessment:

Temporal Relationship
A reasonable temporal relationship was present.
The adverse reaction developed approximately 48 hours after ciprofloxacin was initiated.

Dechallenge
The patient's symptoms substantially improved after ciprofloxacin was discontinued.
Positive dechallenge supports a relationship between the drug and reaction.

Rechallenge
Rechallenge was not performed, so there is no evidence of recurrence after re-exposure.

Alternative Causes
No strong alternative cause was identified from the case information.

Known Drug-Event Relationship
Gastrointestinal adverse effects such as nausea, vomiting and abdominal discomfort are recognized with ciprofloxacin.

Final Causality Classification

Classification: PROBABLE
The reaction is classified as Probable because:
There is a reasonable temporal relationship between ciprofloxacin exposure and onset of the reaction. The reaction improved after withdrawal of ciprofloxacin.
No strong alternative cause was identified.
The reaction is consistent with a known adverse effect of the suspected drug.
Rechallenge was not performed.

The case is not classified as Certain because there was no deliberate or documented unintentional rechallenge demonstrating recurrence of the reaction.

Final Assessment
Suspected Drug:Ciprofloxacin
Adverse Reaction:Nausea, vomiting and abdominal discomfort

Temporal Relationship:Present
Dechallenge:Positive
Rechallenge:Not performed
Alternative Cause:No strong alternative cause identified
Causality Classification:PROBABLE

Conclusion:
Based on the available clinical information, the adverse reaction is considered probably related to ciprofloxacin.
